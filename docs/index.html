🍇 Grapes Ready for Picking: Investigating the Readiness of Precision Viticulture Datasets for Scene Reconstruction

Abstract

Public datasets of vineyard imagery were collected for detection, segmentation and disease mapping, and they are now being proposed as a source for 3D scene reconstruction. The properties that reconstruction depends on, such as view overlap, parallax and frame spacing, are often omitted from their documentation, so it is unclear which of these datasets can be reconstructed. The aim of this study is to measure that for every publicly available vineyard dataset. A structured search identifies eighteen datasets, of which ten contain overlapping views. These ten are characterized under a schema of thirty-six documented and measured properties, and every sequence that registers is reconstructed with one fixed pipeline of structure from motion followed by radiance field reconstruction via 3D Gaussian Splatting, giving twenty-eight reconstructions on two held-out splits. Registration is decided by the capture rather than by the dataset, novel-view quality varies as much within a dataset as between datasets, and frame spacing is the one capture property that limits reconstruction. For the datasets that fail to register or that reconstruct with visible artifacts, three preprocessing steps are tested as ablations. Of these three steps, one restores registration, one improves rendered quality only marginally, and one, which masks static content that contaminates the reconstruction, exposes a fault in the camera motion that masking alone cannot fix. The schema and the pipeline give a procedure that can be applied to any vineyard dataset, existing or new, and the results are published as an open online catalog meant to help researchers choose data and to grow as further datasets are assessed in the same way.
